Parent Information

Everything you need to know about our nursery and how we operate.

Starting Nursery

Before your child starts, we will talk with you about their routines, interests, care needs and the best way to support their transition into nursery.

Settling-in visits help children become familiar with their room, their key person and the adults who will care for them. We will work with you to share updates and support the move from home into the nursery day.

There are currently no spaces available, with the next expected availability in August 2027. To ask about future spaces or arrange a visit, use the Book a Visit page.

A Typical Day At Nursery

Children benefit from a balance of familiar routines, play, rest and outdoor time across the day. A typical day may include:

1

Arrival and settling in

Children are welcomed into the room and supported to settle into the day.

2

Morning play and activities

Planned and child-led activities help build confidence, language and curiosity.

3

Snack and lunch

Mealtimes are part of the nursery routine and support independence and social skills.

4

Outdoor play

Children enjoy fresh air, movement and exploration in our enclosed outdoor areas.

5

Rest and quiet time

Quiet activities and rest are built around each child’s age and individual needs.

6

Stories, songs and home time

The day ends with familiar routines and handovers to families.

Meals, Sleep And Routines

We work with families to understand each child’s dietary needs, sleep routines, personal care and comfort arrangements. Please share changes to your child’s routine so that staff can keep their care consistent and responsive.

Healthy Screen Habits

Young children learn best through warm interaction, reading, play and conversation. We keep screen use limited, purposeful and supervised, and prioritise screen-free time for meals, sleep, movement and play.

  • Under two years: avoid screen time except for shared activities that encourage bonding and interaction.
  • Ages two to five: aim for no more than one hour a day, and less if possible.
  • Choose slow-paced, age-appropriate content and avoid social media, unsuitable material and AI toys or chatbots.
  • Watch and discuss content together rather than leaving young children to use screens alone.

Read our Screen Use Policy or visit the government screen-use guidance.
